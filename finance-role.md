**Language level: CEFR B1–B2 — simple spoken English, with the correct finance terms.**

## Cashbook & TIMS | What does Cashbook do, what is TIMS, and how do the two systems fit together?

### Answer

Cashbook is a financial software company. Its software helps businesses automate finance processes such as **Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable and Bank Reconciliation**.

**TIMS means Total Information Management System.** It is an **ERP system** from Computers Unlimited. An ERP is the main business system a company can use to manage areas such as finance, accounting, purchasing, inventory and operations.

For a TIMS customer, I think of it like this:

**TIMS holds the customer and accounting records → Cashbook brings in bank and remittance information → Cashbook matches and automates the finance work → the results can be posted back into TIMS.**

So Cashbook does not replace the whole ERP. It works with TIMS and helps automate finance processes around it.

## Core Finance Processes | Can you explain bank reconciliation, cash application and collections, and how they relate to Accounts Receivable?

### Answer

**Bank reconciliation** means comparing the company’s accounting records with the bank statement and checking that they agree. If they do not agree, you identify the difference, investigate it and either correct it or explain it.

**Cash application** is what happens when a customer pays. The payment has to be matched to the correct customer account and the correct invoice or invoices. Remittance information, invoice numbers, payment amounts and customer references can help with that matching.

**Collections** deals with money that customers still owe. It means keeping track of unpaid invoices, following up where necessary and helping the business collect the money that is due.

They are connected. A customer invoice creates an **Accounts Receivable balance**. When the customer pays, cash application matches the payment to that invoice. Bank reconciliation checks that the bank movement agrees with the accounting records. If an invoice is still unpaid, it may move into the collections process.

## AP & AR | What is the difference between Accounts Payable and Accounts Receivable, and where would Cashbook’s automation add value?

### Answer

I remember it as **AR is money coming in and AP is money going out**.

**Accounts Receivable, or AR,** is money that customers owe the business. If I sell something to a customer on credit and they have not paid yet, that amount is a receivable.

**Accounts Payable, or AP,** is money the business owes to suppliers. If a supplier sends the company an invoice and it has not been paid yet, that amount is a payable.

Automation can reduce manual work on both sides. On the AR side, it can help match customer payments to invoices, update records and support collections. On the AP side, it can help manage supplier invoices, approvals and payments.

The main value is **less manual processing, fewer errors, faster matching and better visibility of what has been paid and what is still outstanding**.

## Lockbox & Remittance | What is a lockbox, what is remittance information, and how does that information help automate cash application?

### Answer

A **lockbox** is a payment service where a bank receives and processes customer payments on behalf of a business. The bank can then provide the business with a file containing the payment information.

**Remittance information** explains what the payment is for. It may contain the customer name, invoice number, amount, payment date or other references.

That information is extremely useful for cash application because receiving €10,000 into a bank account does not automatically tell you which invoices the customer intended to pay.

Cashbook can bring together the **bank payment, the lockbox information, the remittance and the open invoices**. It can then use matching rules, and in some cases OCR, to automatically match payments to invoices.

If the system is confident, it can automate the match. If something is unclear — for example a partial payment or missing reference — it can leave it as an **exception for a person to review**.

## ERP & Data Formats | What is an ERP system, and why do formats such as EDI, BAI, MT940, CSV or XML matter when financial systems exchange data?

### Answer

An **ERP — Enterprise Resource Planning system —** is a central business system that brings areas such as finance, accounting, purchasing, inventory and operations together.

TIMS is the ERP system relevant to this role.

Cashbook also has to receive information from banks and customers. That information can arrive in different formats.

**EDI** is Electronic Data Interchange — structured electronic information exchanged between businesses.

**BAI2** is a bank reporting format used to exchange account and transaction information.

**MT940** is a standard electronic bank-statement format associated with SWIFT and is widely used in Europe.

**CSV and XML** are more general data formats that systems can also use to exchange structured information.

I do not think the important thing for this placement is memorising every field in every format. The important idea is that **different systems may describe the same financial activity in different structures, and the implementation has to map that information correctly so it can be processed and matched reliably**.

## Implementation Associate | What do you think you would actually be doing day to day as a TIMS Implementation Associate, and how would you help a customer increase automation?

### Answer

I see the role as a mixture of **finance, technology and customer work**.

I would support the TIMS implementation team with daily implementation and support work, while learning the basics of **cash application, bank reconciliation and collections**. I would also help with **lockbox development and application**.

A big part of the role is working with customers to increase the amount of bank reconciliation that can be automated. That means understanding their finance process, understanding the data coming from TIMS and the banks, helping test the matching rules and looking at the transactions that do not match automatically.

The job specification also mentions **Microsoft SQL, Excel, troubleshooting application errors, communicating with customers and liaising with IT to debug problems**.

So I would not describe it as just a finance role or just a coding role. It is a **junior business-consulting and implementation role**: understand the customer’s process, understand the data, help configure and test the solution, explain problems clearly and keep improving the automation without sacrificing accuracy.

# Quick Glossary

| Term | Easy meaning |
|---|---|
| **AP — Accounts Payable** | Money the business owes to suppliers. |
| **AR — Accounts Receivable** | Money customers owe to the business. |
| **Bank reconciliation** | Compare the bank statement with the company’s records and investigate differences. |
| **Cash application** | Match an incoming customer payment to the correct account and invoice or invoices. |
| **Collections** | Manage and follow up money that customers still owe. |
| **TIMS** | **Total Information Management System** — the ERP system from Computers Unlimited used by customers supported by the TIMS team. |
| **ERP** | **Enterprise Resource Planning** — a central system linking business functions such as finance, accounting, purchasing, inventory and operations. |
| **Lockbox** | A bank service that receives and processes customer payments for a business and provides payment information back to the business. |
| **Remittance** | Information explaining what a payment is for, such as invoice numbers and amounts. |
| **GL — General Ledger** | The main accounting record containing the company’s financial transactions and balances. |
| **Exception** | A transaction that cannot be handled confidently by the automatic rules and needs review. |
| **Automation** | Using software and rules to complete repeatable work with less manual processing. |
| **EDI** | **Electronic Data Interchange** — structured electronic information exchanged between organisations. |
| **BAI2** | A standard bank reporting file format used for account and transaction information. |
| **MT940** | A standard electronic bank-statement format associated with SWIFT. |
| **SWIFT** | **Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication** — a network and standards system used for financial messaging. |
| **CSV** | **Comma-Separated Values** — a simple tabular text-file format. |
| **XML** | **Extensible Markup Language** — a structured text format used to exchange data between systems. |
| **OCR** | **Optical Character Recognition** — technology that turns text in images or scanned documents into machine-readable data. |
| **SQL** | **Structured Query Language** — used to query and work with data stored in relational databases. |
