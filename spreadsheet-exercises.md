# Spreadsheet Exercises & Skills

## Key Line

**The point of these exercises is not to memorise formulas. It is to show that I can use spreadsheets to make financial and operational information accurate, controlled, explainable and useful.**

This project is a structured practice workbook built around the type of spreadsheet work that appears in corporate services and financial operations.

[Open the current practice workbook →](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1nrBBSbxHZ3jOyKf868grsnVoFMlXPyEOdLIBb8tRNy4/edit?usp=drivesdk)

## What I am practising

- invoice arithmetic, VAT and gross calculations
- accounting-style opening/closing balance logic
- IF, AND, OR and exception controls
- XLOOKUP / VLOOKUP record matching
- SUMIFS and COUNTIFS reconciliations
- data cleaning, standardisation and duplicate checks
- quarter and rate-based reporting summaries
- pivot-table thinking and dashboard preparation

The workbook also contains independent checks. A correct result can produce a green tick, while an incorrect or incomplete result stays blank. This gives feedback without replacing the need to understand the task.

## What the skills mean in a job

**Accuracy and control** — I can build checks around calculations instead of assuming that a formula is correct.

**Reconciliation** — I can compare expected and actual records, explain differences and isolate exceptions that need investigation.

**Data quality** — I can clean names, references and other identifiers before matching or reporting.

**Reporting support** — I can aggregate detailed transaction data into controlled summaries and trace totals back to source records.

**Process thinking** — I can recognise repetitive manual steps and consider where validation or automation could reduce error risk.

## How I would explain it in an interview

A good explanation is:

> **Purpose → what I did → control/check → exception or decision → what I learned.**

For example:

> “I reconciled invoice and payment records, checked that each balance could be explained and flagged unmatched items for review. The main lesson was that a spreadsheet formula is only useful if the underlying records and controls are reliable.”

That is stronger than simply saying that I used **SUMIFS** or **XLOOKUP**.
