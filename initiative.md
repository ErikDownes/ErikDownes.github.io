## Improve a Process | Tell me about a time you noticed a process could be improved and took the initiative to do something about it.

At O’Mahony’s, I was working from printed Booksolve order reports, which were really only point-in-time snapshots and could become outdated as information changed. When an inconsistency contributed to books being sent to the wrong customer, I helped resolve the immediate issue with both customers, but then asked my line manager if we could look critically at why it had happened. I suggested reviewing whether appropriate Booksolve access and a refreshed export into Excel could give us more current information rather than relying so heavily on paper. What interested me was moving beyond fixing one mistake and looking at how the process itself could be made more reliable.

## Learn Quickly | Tell me about a time you had to learn a new tool, system or process quickly in order to get something done.

### Situation

I was working on a data-analysis assignment using historical Dublin Bikes data and reached a point where information from different periods would not line up properly because the date and timestamp formats were inconsistent.

### Task

I had a deadline, so manually fixing individual records was not realistic. I needed to learn quickly how to deal with dates and times properly in pandas so that I could make the data consistent and continue the analysis.

### Action

In my Dublin Bikes project, eight years of data contained changing date and timestamp formats — abbreviated and full month names, two-digit and four-digit years, and dates stored as text. With around 55 million rows, that cannot be corrected manually.

With larger datasets, pandas and DataFrames let me apply the same logic across whole columns, while tools such as `to_datetime` can normalise inconsistent timestamps. I learned how to parse the different formats, test the output and make sure the dates were being interpreted consistently before moving on to the analysis.

### Result

Once the timestamps were standardised, I could combine the historical data properly and continue with the station-level and time-of-day analysis.

### What I learned

When I meet a technical gap under time pressure, I try to **identify exactly what I do not know, learn the specific tool I need, test it on the real data and apply it immediately** rather than trying to learn everything at once.

## Find Another Way | Tell me about a time your original plan was no longer going to work and you took action to find another way forward.

### Situation

In the 2024 Higher Level Applied Maths paper, I worked through a business-planning problem where a company had different promotional strategies available over four years, with different profits and losses attached to each route.

### Task

Looking at the whole network at once made the problem complicated, so I needed to reduce it into smaller decisions while keeping the final objective clear — **maximising total profit**.

### Action

I used **Bellman’s Principle of Optimality**. Instead of trying every complete route from the start, I worked backwards from the final year.

At each stage I calculated the best outcome available from that point onwards, recorded it, and then used that result when analysing the previous stage. That turned one large problem into a sequence of smaller, manageable decisions.

### Result

By working backwards systematically, I could identify the optimal route through the network rather than relying on trial and error.

### What I learned

When a problem looks too large, I try to **define the objective, break it into smaller components, solve them in a logical order and then bring the results back together**.

## Take Ownership | Tell me about a time you took responsibility for something and followed it through to completion without needing to be reminded.

### Situation

At O’Mahony’s, part of the difficulty with checking orders was that I was often working from printed Booksolve reports rather than the most current information in the system.

### Task

Once I had identified that as a weakness, I did not want to leave it as something somebody else might eventually deal with. I wanted to get the appropriate Booksolve access so I could check the current information properly while still respecting the company’s access controls.

### Action

I raised it with my line manager, explained why the access would help me do the checking more reliably and followed through on getting the appropriate software access rather than just mentioning the idea once.

Once I had access, I learned enough of the system to use the current information as part of my checks instead of relying only on printed snapshots.

### Result

It gave me a more direct way to verify current order information and meant I could take more responsibility for the accuracy of my own work.

### What I learned

For me, ownership means **noticing what would help, raising it properly, following it through and then actually using the solution responsibly once it is available**.
