The **Interview** area is organised around reusable domains that apply across employers. The aim is to build strong answers once, then adapt the final part to the role.

## Motivation & Role Fit

Explain why the work is interesting, what you can contribute and what you want to learn from the placement.

# Motivation & Role Fit

## Key Line

**Explain what interests you, connect it to evidence from your degree or experience, and finish by showing where you want to develop.**

## Why did you choose Financial Mathematics?

### Build your answer

Try answering this aloud in **30–45 seconds**.

Your answer should contain three things:

- **Interest:** What attracted you to mathematics, finance, data or problem-solving?
- **Evidence:** What parts of the degree have confirmed that interest?
- **Direction:** Why does that make a professional finance or technology role attractive now?

### Self-check

After answering, ask:

- Did I actually explain **why**, rather than just describe the course?
- Did I mention something specific I genuinely enjoy?
- Did I connect mathematics to a **real financial or business purpose**?
- Could I say it naturally without sounding learned off?

### Useful recall

**Maths → real decisions → finance → application**

---

## What do you enjoy most about Financial Mathematics?

Do not simply say **“I like maths.”**

Choose one or two areas and explain what you like about them:

- solving structured problems;
- using data to understand what is happening;
- applying mathematics to financial decisions;
- modelling uncertainty and risk;
- programming or analysing data;
- getting from a complicated problem to a clear answer.

### Self-check

A strong answer should include:

**Interest → example → why it matters**

---

## Why do you want a placement like this?

Separate this from **“Why this company?”**

Explain why the **type of work** appeals to you.

Possible themes:

- applying university learning to real business problems;
- working with financial information and processes;
- solving problems where accuracy matters;
- communicating with colleagues or customers rather than working only on theoretical problems;
- learning how professional teams actually operate.

### Recall

**Apply → contribute → learn**

---

## What can you contribute to the placement?

Give evidence rather than adjectives.

Instead of:

> “I am analytical and hardworking.”

Think:

- **Analytical:** mathematics, statistics, financial mathematics, data analysis.
- **Technical:** Excel, Python, programming and working with data.
- **Accurate:** checking information, reconciliations and detail-focused work.
- **Communication:** explaining problems and working with colleagues or customers.
- **Teamwork:** college projects and employment experience.

### Self-check

Could the interviewer ask **“Can you give me an example?”** and could you answer immediately?

---

## What do you want to learn from the placement?

Avoid saying only **“I want experience.”**

Be more specific:

- understand how financial processes operate in a real organisation;
- see how software, data and finance interact;
- improve professional communication with colleagues and customers;
- learn how experienced staff investigate and solve operational problems;
- understand where your Financial Mathematics degree can add value in practice.

### Recall

**Real systems → professional judgement → greater responsibility**

---

## Assessment as Learning

For each question:

**Attempt it → check it → improve it → answer it again.**

Score your first answer out of 4:

- **1 — Direct:** Did I answer the actual question?
- **1 — Evidence:** Did I give something concrete?
- **1 — Relevance:** Did I connect it to work?
- **1 — Natural:** Does it sound like me?

**Target: 4/4 in under 45 seconds.**

Do not learn a paragraph word-for-word. Learn the **four or five ideas that reconstruct the answer**.

## Communication

Clear speaking, active listening, professional written communication and checking understanding.

## Problem-Solving

Understand the problem, identify the important information, work methodically, test the result and explain the reasoning.

## Accuracy & Attention to Detail

Check figures, reconcile information, follow the required process and resolve exceptions rather than forcing an answer.

## Teamwork

Work reliably with other people, communicate dependencies, respond to feedback and help the team deliver.

## Customer Care

Understand what the customer or internal client needs, communicate clearly, take ownership and follow through.

## Organisation & Priorities

Manage competing tasks, identify deadlines and dependencies, and communicate early when priorities or timing change.
