**Cook it. Taste it. Change something next time.**

Cooking is something I enjoy regularly, and often daily. I probably put more effort into making a meal than I strictly need to, but that is part of the enjoyment.

What I like most is that cooking has a very obvious feedback loop. I try something, taste the result, notice what worked and what did not, and change it the next time. There is no point pretending every attempt is perfect. If I oversalt something, there is only so much I can do about it — and I am probably still going to eat it — but I will remember it the next time.

## Bolognese | The one I keep changing

Spaghetti bolognese is probably the dish I have experimented with most.

I like to cook the onions slowly for quite a long time rather than rushing them. I use tinned tomatoes and tomato purée because I like the sauce to stay moist but still have some depth. I also like getting proper colour on the meat at a high temperature rather than simply letting it cook in the sauce.

Over time I have started to notice why certain ingredients work together rather than just following a recipe.

One of the ideas I discovered through cooking is **umami** — the deep savoury taste you get from foods such as tomatoes, Parmesan, meat, mushrooms and some fish. Once I started noticing it, I began thinking more about balance and flavour rather than just quantities.

## Roast chicken | Simple things done properly

I also make a pretty good roast chicken.

The potatoes are a large part of it. I like cooking them twice so they end up soft inside and properly crisp on the outside. It is not complicated, but small changes in timing, heat and preparation make a big difference.

That is probably what I like about cooking generally. A recipe gives me a starting point, but repetition is what teaches me what actually works.

## Eating out | Food is social too

I also appreciate good food in restaurants.

For me, eating out is not just about analysing what is on the plate. I enjoy the social side of it: sitting around a table, having a conversation and sharing a meal with other people.

Good service matters as well. I like the balance where somebody is professional and knows what they are doing, but the interaction still feels natural and friendly.

## Blog | Photographing the process

Now that I have a blog, I might start taking photographs of some of the meals I make.

Not because every dinner needs to become content, but because it would be interesting to have a record of things I tried, what changed and what I eventually learned to do well.
