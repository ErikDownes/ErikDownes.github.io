## Pause Before You Answer | The most important pause

<div class="home-performance">
  <div class="home-performance-line">Hear the question.</div>
  <div class="home-performance-line">Pause.</div>
  <div class="home-performance-line home-performance-line-strong">Then answer.</div>
  <p>
    A short silence is not a failure. It gives you time to choose the point you actually want to make before the words begin.
    <strong>Two or three seconds is normal. Up to about five seconds is fine for a substantial question.</strong>
  </p>
</div>

<div class="home-three">
  <div class="home-panel">
    <span class="home-panel-number">01</span>
    <strong>Do not fill the silence</strong>
    <p>No “em”, no verbal runway and no automatic “I think”. Start with the answer.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="home-panel">
    <span class="home-panel-number">02</span>
    <strong>One thought at a time</strong>
    <p>Do not thought-hop. Finish one idea before moving to another.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="home-panel">
    <span class="home-panel-number">03</span>
    <strong>Stop when it is answered</strong>
    <p>Do not keep talking because silence feels uncomfortable. Let the interviewer take the next turn.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="practice-rule home-practice">
  <strong>Default structure:</strong> Point → evidence → relevance → stop.
</div>

<div class="home-dont">
  <span>NEVER OPEN WITH “I THINK”.</span>
  <strong>Make the statement. Then support it.</strong>
</div>

## 40 · 60 · 80 Seconds | Control the length

<p class="home-section-lead">
  Learn the same material at different lengths. The aim is not a fixed speech — it is the ability to expand or contract a prepared answer to fit the question.
</p>

<div class="home-three">
  <div class="home-panel">
    <span class="home-panel-number">40</span>
    <strong>Short</strong>
    <p>Answer directly. One clear point and one useful piece of evidence.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="home-panel">
    <span class="home-panel-number">60</span>
    <strong>Normal</strong>
    <p>Add enough context and explanation to make the answer convincing.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="home-panel">
    <span class="home-panel-number">80</span>
    <strong>Full</strong>
    <p>Use the extra time only when the question genuinely needs more detail.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="practice-rule home-practice">
  <strong>The drill:</strong> Hear the question → pause → answer it in 40 seconds → repeat at 60 → repeat at 80.
</div>

## Practice Questions | Start from silence

<p class="home-section-lead">
  Do not write a fresh essay for every question. Use these as retrieval prompts. Pause first, choose the point, then speak.
</p>

<details>
  <summary><strong>Tell me about yourself.</strong></summary>
  <p>Course → strongest evidence → why this placement. Aim for about 60 seconds.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>Why are you interested in Abelo and aircraft asset management?</strong></summary>
  <p>Company → turboprop leasing → analytical work → what you want to learn.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>Tell me about a project you are proud of.</strong></summary>
  <p>Problem → your contribution → method → result → what you learned.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>Tell me about a time something did not go to plan.</strong></summary>
  <p>What changed → what you did → judgement → outcome.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>How do you approach a problem when you do not know the answer?</strong></summary>
  <p>Clarify → break it down → investigate → verify → ask for help when useful.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>Explain something technical in simple language.</strong></summary>
  <p>One idea → plain-English explanation → example → check understanding.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>How do you make sure your work is accurate?</strong></summary>
  <p>Method → checks → reconciliation → assumptions → escalation if something does not make sense.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary><strong>Why should we choose you for this placement?</strong></summary>
  <p>Evidence, not adjectives: mathematical training → data skills → curiosity → willingness to learn.</p>
</details>

### Why I like it

<p>
  The power is in the <strong>storytelling, prompts and simple heuristics</strong>. Winston gives the speaker things to do rather than vague advice about “confidence”: cycle back to an idea, build a fence around a concept, use props, use the board and control the opening and finish.
</p>

<p>
  One small example is the act of <strong>pointing</strong>. Even a simple physical cue can direct human attention and help an audience track the speaker. It is a reminder that communication is not just the words — delivery changes what people notice and remember.
</p>

<div class="practice-rule home-practice">
  <strong>For interviews:</strong> do not memorise a speech. Build prompts that help you retrieve the story, make the point and stop.
</div>

### Speaking and writing in the AI age

<p>
  The ability to communicate still means being able to <strong>speak clearly</strong>. The ability to write now also includes recognising good writing, prompting well, iterating, editing and judging the result.
</p>

<p>
  AI is a fantastic writing tool. It makes writing judgement more important, not less: you still need to know what you mean, what to keep, what to cut and what you are willing to stand over.
</p>

<div class="home-dont">
  <span>USE AI TO ITERATE.</span>
  <strong>Keep the judgement human.</strong>
</div>

<details>
  <summary><strong>Language worth noticing</strong></summary>
  <p>Alliteration, assonance, rhythm, analogy, contrast and precision can make an idea easier to retrieve and easier to hear. The point is not decoration. The point is clarity, recall and confidence.</p>
</details>
