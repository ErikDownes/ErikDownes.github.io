## Teamwork

**Listen · Contribute · Deliver**

I work best in teams when everyone understands the shared goal, communicates clearly and takes responsibility for their part.

At Mr Price, I worked as part of a large evening team drawn from different shifts to reorganise the shop floor. I adapted to changing tasks, worked with colleagues I did not normally work with and helped complete the reset so the store was ready for normal trading.

In CE4702, I completed Java programming assignments as pair work. We divided tasks, discussed problems, reviewed each other's work and integrated the final code. The important part was not just producing a working submission, but making sure both of us understood the overall solution.

## Communication Skills

**Listen · Understand · Connect**

Communication is much more than speaking clearly. It includes listening, tone of voice, body language, eye contact, appearance, visual presentation, written accuracy and understanding how another person is likely to receive a message.

In a global workplace, people may have different professional, cultural and technical backgrounds, so good communication means adapting without losing clarity. In an Irish workplace such as Shannon, warmth, approachability and ordinary people skills also matter. I see communication as partly psychological: first understand the person, the context and what they need, then decide how best to communicate.

At O'Mahony's, I communicate with colleagues across servicing, packing and dispatch so books, invoices, box counts and library destinations match. At Mr Price, customer-facing work developed the ability to listen, answer questions clearly and deal with problems calmly and respectfully.

My project work also develops visual communication: presenting data, charts, maps and technical information so that somebody else can understand the result rather than simply seeing the calculation.

## Problem Solving and Analytics

**Define · Analyse · Explain**

I try to define the real problem before choosing a method or tool.

In MS4034, I worked with a used-car dataset, cleaned the data, tested predictors and fitted a regression model. Diagnostics identified outliers and non-linearity; a log transformation and polynomial mileage term improved explanatory power from **21% to 84%**.

My aircraft fleet project required a different type of problem solving. Public records were incomplete and sometimes inconsistent, so I compared sources, preserved unresolved cases where the evidence was insufficient and built a structured view of aircraft, lessees and locations.

I use mathematics, statistics and programming to analyse a problem, but the final step is always to explain what the result means.

## Using Initiative

**Notice · Investigate · Improve**

I use initiative by noticing something that could be improved and then taking responsibility for investigating it.

At Mr Price, after resolving a customer's missing-price query, I noticed that the shelf label itself was absent and replaced it so the same problem would not recur.

In Applied Mathematics, I extended a rollercoaster model beyond the syllabus by independently researching air resistance, deriving the relevant relationships and incorporating drag into the model. The project achieved **86%**.

I also use independent projects to learn technologies and methods that are useful beyond a single module or assignment.

## Projects, Portfolio and Volunteering

**Build · Understand · Contribute**

I use projects to go beyond simply using an existing tool or seeing its final output. I want to understand the data and process underneath it.

**Aircraft Fleet and Asset Management** — I reconciled public aircraft records across sources and built filterable views by aircraft, lessee, country and region.

**GPX Ride Analysis — in development** — Strava already does the heavy lifting, but as a data-analysis student I wanted to work with the underlying GPX data myself. The project extracts coordinates, time and elevation data and uses them to recreate and understand parts of the ride analysis.

**iPhone Product Scanner — in development** — a phone-first web project that scans a product barcode, retrieves structured product information and explores what useful data can be extracted, checked and analysed. The point is not simply to reproduce a commercial scanner, but to understand the data pipeline behind it.

**Volunteering — next step** — I am on the waiting list for volunteering opportunities and am contacting the UL Student Volunteering Office to offer my time and ask where I can contribute. I want volunteering to give me useful experience of working with different people, contributing to a community and being part of a team outside work and university assignments.
