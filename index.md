## Articulation Practice | Say it clearly

<p class="home-section-lead">
  Articulation is not about sounding elaborate. It is about making the answer easy to follow, easy to remember and easy to say.
</p>

## 40 · 60 · 80 Seconds | Control the length

<p class="home-section-lead">
  Learn the same material at different lengths. The aim is not a fixed speech — it is the ability to expand or contract a prepared answer to fit the question.
</p>

<div class="home-three">
  <div class="home-panel">
    <span class="home-panel-number">40</span>
    <strong>Short</strong>
    <p>Answer directly. One clear point and one useful piece of evidence.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="home-panel">
    <span class="home-panel-number">60</span>
    <strong>Normal</strong>
    <p>Add enough context and explanation to make the answer convincing.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="home-panel">
    <span class="home-panel-number">80</span>
    <strong>Full</strong>
    <p>Use the extra time only when the question genuinely needs more detail.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="practice-rule home-practice">
  <strong>The drill:</strong> Answer the same question in 40 seconds, then 60, then 80.
</div>

## Pause | Knowing when to stop

<div class="home-performance">
  <div class="home-performance-line">Answer.</div>
  <div class="home-performance-line">Pause.</div>
  <div class="home-performance-line home-performance-line-strong">Stop.</div>
  <p>
    Once you have answered the question, <strong>stop talking.</strong>
    Give the interviewer room to ask the next question or a follow-up.
    Pausing and stopping are as important as articulation.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="home-dont">
  <span>PREPARE MORE THAN YOU SAY.</span>
  <strong>Say only what the question needs.</strong>
</div>

  </div>

<div class="home-performance">
  <div class="home-performance-line">Actors rehearse.</div>
  <div class="home-performance-line">Broadcasters repeat.</div>
  <div class="home-performance-line home-performance-line-strong">Candidates should too.</div>
  <p>A person appearing on several radio or television interviews does not invent a completely new message on every programme. They know their points. They know their examples. They have said them before. <strong>That is interview preparation.</strong></p>

  <strong>Build the wording first.</strong>

<div class="home-skills">

  <div><strong>STRUCTURE</strong><span>Know where the answer is going before you start speaking.</span></div>
  <div><strong>EVIDENCE</strong><span>Use projects, modules, work and STAR examples instead of empty claims.</span></div>
  <div><strong>CONNECTION</strong><span>Link your course, strengths and interests directly to the company and role.</span></div>
  <div><strong>JUDGEMENT</strong><span>Show that you understand trade-offs, constraints and real workplace problems.</span></div>

<div class="practice-rule home-practice"><strong>The method:</strong> Develop the answer → edit it → settle it → say it aloud → say it again. Do not keep reinventing it.</div>

Language worth noticing

Erik does not need to sound ornate. He should start noticing why some language is easier to remember and easier to say.

Useful things to notice include:





alliteration — repeated starting sounds



assonance — repeated vowel sounds



rhythm — sentence shape that makes a phrase easier to retrieve



analogy — explaining an unfamiliar idea through a familiar one



contrast — setting two ideas beside each other so the distinction becomes clear



precision — choosing the word that says exactly what is meant

The point is not decoration. The point is clarity, recall and confidence.
