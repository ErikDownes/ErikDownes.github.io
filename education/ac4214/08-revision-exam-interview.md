## Exam habits | The lecturer's own warning

The exam-details material explicitly says to **label numbers** rather than leaving unexplained figures on the page. That is good professional practice too: a reviewer should be able to see what a number represents and where it came from.

For calculation questions:

1. write the formula/decision rule;
2. label units, years and cash-flow timing;
3. show workings in a readable sequence;
4. state the numerical conclusion;
5. add the decision/recommendation;
6. include qualitative factors when requested.

## Topic map | What the papers repeatedly demand

The 2023/2024 material and revision packs repeatedly draw on the same core families:

- relevant costs and qualitative decision factors;
- contribution, break-even, target profit and margin of safety;
- scarce-resource / limiting-factor optimisation;
- sales, purchases and cash budgets;
- payback, ARR, NPV and IRR / investment judgement;
- working capital, EOQ/JIT, inventory/receivable days and credit policy.

The context changes — products, events, contracts, inventory and projects — but the underlying method should be recognisable.

## Interview transfer | 20 seconds, 60 seconds, then evidence

### 20-second version

> “AC4214 was a practical management-accounting and finance module. It taught me how to identify the cash flows that matter, understand contribution and break-even, allocate scarce resources, budget cash, appraise investments and manage working capital.”

### 60-second version

> “Accounting for Financial Decision Making was useful because it connected accounting numbers to actual choices. We covered relevant costing, CVP and break-even, constrained-resource decisions, budgeting and cash flow, investment appraisal using Payback, ARR, NPV and IRR, and working-capital decisions such as EOQ and receivables. The main lesson I retain is to identify which cash flows genuinely change, make the assumptions explicit, run the calculation correctly and then consider qualitative risks before recommending a decision.”

### Evidence if challenged

Be ready to produce one concrete example:

- explain why replacement cost/opportunity cost can matter more than historic cost;
- calculate break-even from price, variable cost and fixed cost;
- rank products by contribution per scarce hour;
- map a cash collection pattern across months;
- explain why NPV discounts cash;
- evaluate whether an early-payment discount is financially worthwhile.
