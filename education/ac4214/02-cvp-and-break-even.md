## Contribution | The engine of CVP

<button data-ac-term="cvp analysis">CVP analysis</button> focuses on selling price, volume, variable cost, total fixed cost and — for multiple products — sales mix.

The key quantity is <button data-ac-term="contribution">contribution</button>:

<div class="ac-formula">
Contribution per unit = Selling price per unit − Variable cost per unit

Profit = Total contribution − Fixed costs
</div>

Contribution is **not profit**. It is what remains after variable cost to cover fixed cost and then profit.

## Break-even | Zero profit, zero loss

The <button data-ac-term="break-even point">break-even point</button> occurs when contribution exactly equals fixed cost.

<div class="ac-formula">
Break-even units = Fixed costs ÷ Contribution per unit
</div>

The archive teaches a second route — the equation method:

**Sales revenue − Variable costs − Fixed costs = 0**

<div class="ac-worked">
<strong>Kemmy calculators — lecture example</strong>
<p>Selling price €35, variable cost €20, contribution €15, fixed cost €45,000.</p>
<p>Break-even units = €45,000 ÷ €15 = <strong>3,000 calculators</strong>.</p>
<p>Check: 3,000 × €35 − 3,000 × €20 − €45,000 = €0.</p>
</div>

<div class="ac-mcq" data-id="cvp-be" data-answer="1" data-explain="Contribution is €130 per unit and €260,000 ÷ €130 = 2,000 units.">

## Target profit | Add the profit requirement

<div class="ac-formula">
Units for target profit = (Fixed costs + Target profit) ÷ Contribution per unit
</div>

<div class="ac-worked">
<strong>Allegro meals — lecture example</strong>
<p>Selling price €22, variable cost €12, contribution €10, fixed cost €25,000, target profit €15,000.</p>
<p>Required units = (€25,000 + €15,000) ÷ €10 = <strong>4,000 meals</strong>.</p>
</div>

## Margin of safety | How much room is there?

<button data-ac-term="margin of safety">Margin of safety</button> measures how far actual or budgeted sales sit above break-even.

<div class="ac-formula">
Margin of safety units = Actual/budgeted units − Break-even units

Margin of safety % = Margin of safety ÷ Actual/budgeted sales × 100
</div>

The lecture's horse-box example has break-even of 50,000 and budgeted sales of 60,000: margin of safety = 10,000 units = 16.7%.

## Contribution-to-sales ratio | When sales value matters

The solution pack also uses the contribution-to-sales ratio:

<div class="ac-formula">
C/S ratio = Contribution ÷ Sales revenue

Break-even sales revenue = Fixed costs ÷ C/S ratio
</div>

This is especially useful when the question asks for revenue rather than units.

## Assumptions | Know when CVP can mislead

The exam solutions explicitly require limitations/assumptions. CVP usually assumes:

- selling price remains constant;
- variable cost per unit remains constant;
- fixed cost remains fixed within the relevant range;
- total revenue and total cost are linear;
- costs can be split reliably into fixed and variable parts;
- one product or a constant sales mix;
- the analysis applies to the short term / relevant range;
- other variables remain unchanged.

<div class="ac-caution"><strong>Professional judgement:</strong> a break-even number is not a promise. It is the output of assumptions. If price, mix, capacity or cost behaviour changes, the answer changes.</div>
