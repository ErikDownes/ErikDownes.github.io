## Key line | Why corners matter

Each linear inequality defines a half-plane. Their intersection is the <button data-or-term="feasible region">feasible region</button>. For a linear programme, if a finite optimum exists, at least one optimum occurs at an <button data-or-term="extreme point">extreme point</button> of that region.

That fact is the bridge from the graphical method to simplex.

## Method | Graphical solution

For two variables:

1. replace each inequality by equality to draw its boundary;
2. determine which side satisfies the inequality;
3. intersect all allowed half-planes;
4. identify corner points;
5. evaluate the objective at each corner **or** move an iso-profit/iso-cost line parallel to itself;
6. interpret the optimum and check for special cases.

## Iso-lines | A second way to see optimality

For

\[
Z=c_1x_1+c_2x_2,
\]

holding (Z) constant gives a family of parallel lines:

\[
x_2=-\frac{c_1}{c_2}x_1+\frac{Z}{c_2}.
\]

Moving that line in the improving direction until it last touches the feasible region identifies an optimum.

If it touches:

- **one vertex:** unique optimum;
- **an entire edge:** alternate optima;
- **no finite last point while improving:** unbounded objective;
- **no point satisfies every constraint:** infeasible model.

## Special cases | Learn the geometry, not just the labels

**Infeasible** means the constraints contradict one another: the feasible region is empty.

**Unbounded** means the feasible region permits indefinite improvement in the objective direction. It does *not* mean the region itself must be infinite in every direction.

**Redundant constraint** means removing that constraint does not change the feasible region.

**Degeneracy** is an algebraic/vertex phenomenon where more constraints than necessary are active at a basic feasible solution.

## Worked | Corner-point reasoning

Suppose

\[
\max Z=3x+5y
\]

subject to

\[
x\le4,\qquad y\le6,\qquad 3x+2y\le18,\qquad x,y\ge0.
\]

Candidate corners are generated by axis intercepts and intersections of binding constraints. Evaluate (Z) only after confirming that each candidate satisfies **all** constraints.

This “feasible first, objective second” discipline prevents many graphical errors.
