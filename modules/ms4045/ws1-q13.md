<h2>Question 13</h2>
<div class="ms4045-math-click" markdown="1">

Find all solutions. Give both polar and Cartesian forms and sketch some of them in the complex plane.

$$
\text{(a)}\quad e^w=i,
\qquad
\text{(b)}\quad e^w=-1-i.
$$
</div>
</section>

<section class="ms4045-solution-stage" aria-label="Worked solution">
<div class="ms4045-solution-head">
<strong>Worked solution</strong>
<span>Tap below for the next step · tap the question maths above to show all</span>
</div>
<div class="ms4045-solution-body" markdown="1">
<div class="ms4045-rule"><strong>Exponential recipe:</strong> if $e^w=Re^{i\theta}$ and $w=u+iv$, then
$$
u=\ln R,\qquad v=\theta+2\pi k.
$$
So the real part comes from the modulus and the imaginary part comes from the argument.</div>

Write
$$
w=u+iv.
$$
Then
$$
e^w=e^u e^{iv}.
$$

### (a) $e^w=i$

Since
$$
i=e^{i(\pi/2+2\pi k)},
$$
the modulus gives $e^u=1$, hence $u=0$, and
$$
v=\frac\pi2+2\pi k.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{
w_k=i\left(\frac\pi2+2\pi k\right),
\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
}
$$

### (b) $e^w=-1-i$

Now
$$
-1-i=\sqrt2\,e^{i(-3\pi/4+2\pi k)}.
$$
Thus
$$
e^u=\sqrt2
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
u=\frac12\ln2,
$$
and
$$
v=-\frac{3\pi}{4}+2\pi k.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{
w_k=
\frac12\ln2
+i\left(-\frac{3\pi}{4}+2\pi k\right),
\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
}
$$
