Question 1

Assume

$$z=a+ib.$$

Determine whether each equality is correct. For those that are not, give a counterexample and, wherever possible, correct them.

(a) $\operatorname{Re}z=a$
(d) $(z-1)(z-1)^*=(z-1)(z^*+1)$
(b) $\operatorname{Im}z=ib$
(e) $\lvert e^z\rvert=e^{\lvert z\rvert}$
(c) $\lvert z\rvert=\sqrt{a^2+(ib)^2}$
(f) $\lvert e^{iz}\rvert=\sqrt{(e^{iz})^2}$

Tap any expression to reveal the complete worked solution.

Worked solution Tap below to reveal one step at a time.
Keep these four facts visible:
$\operatorname{Re}(a+ib)=a$,   $\operatorname{Im}(a+ib)=b$,   $(a+ib)^*=a-ib$,   $|a+ib|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$.

(a)

✓ Correct.

$$\operatorname{Re}z=\operatorname{Re}(a+ib)=a.$$

(b)

✗ Incorrect. The imaginary part is the real coefficient of $i$, not the whole imaginary term.

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Im}z=b}$$

For example, if $z=1+2i$, then $\operatorname{Im}z=2$, not $2i$.

(c)

✗ Incorrect. Since $(ib)^2=-b^2$, the proposed expression would give the wrong sign.

$$|z|=\sqrt{z\bar z}=\sqrt{(a+ib)(a-ib)}=\boxed{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}.$$

(d)

✗ Incorrect. The star means complex conjugate. It changes the sign of the imaginary part only.

$$(z-1)^*=z^*-1.$$

Therefore

$$(z-1)(z-1)^*=(z-1)(z^*-1)=\boxed{|z-1|^2}.$$

(e)

✗ Incorrect. Write $z=a+ib$:

$$e^z=e^{a+ib}=e^a(\cos b+i\sin b).$$

Hence

$$\boxed{|e^z|=e^a=e^{\operatorname{Re}z}}.$$

Counterexample: for $z=i$, $|e^i|=1$ but $e^{|i|}=e$.

(f)

✗ Incorrect. If $z=a+ib$, then

$$iz=ia-b,$$ $$e^{iz}=e^{-b}e^{ia}.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{|e^{iz}|=e^{-b}=e^{-\operatorname{Im}z}}.$$

In complex arithmetic, $\sqrt{w^2}$ is not generally the same thing as $|w|$.
