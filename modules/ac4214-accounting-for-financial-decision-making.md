## The assessment-for-learning rule

This course does **not** treat reading as learning. Every chapter contains three layers:

1. **Worked understanding** — concepts and calculations are explained from first principles.
2. **Check** — short questions force a decision before the answer is revealed.
3. **Metacognition** — the learner explicitly chooses **Not yet**, **Nearly** or **I know this**.

A chapter is only marked **Mastered** in this browser when its marked checks are correct **and** its confidence gates have been set to **I know this**. Progress is deliberately local to the browser so a new visitor starts fresh.

## Glossary that gets out of the way

Important accounting language is clickable when it first appears. The browser counts encounters. After repeated look-ups, the learner is asked:

**I know this now** · **Keep helping me**

Choosing **I know this now** removes the strong glossary treatment while keeping the term available if clicked again. Choosing **Keep helping me** retains the scaffold. This is learner-controlled fading rather than an arbitrary countdown.

## What the supplied archive actually contains

The upload contained **74 files**: the module outline, lecture decks, tutorial skeletons and worked answers, question/solution PDFs, 2023 and 2024 exam papers with solutions, 2025 mid-term material, revision material, present-value tables and five narrated PowerPoint shows. A small number of unrelated cycling/site files had accidentally travelled inside the ZIP and are excluded from the academic course.

The main assessed knowledge in the archive is:

- classifying costs and identifying <button data-ac-term="relevant cost">relevant costs</button>;
- <button data-ac-term="cvp analysis">CVP analysis</button>, <button data-ac-term="break-even point">break-even</button>, target profit and <button data-ac-term="margin of safety">margin of safety</button>;
- scarce-resource decisions using <button data-ac-term="contribution per limiting factor">contribution per limiting factor</button>;
- budgeting, collections/payments timing and the <button data-ac-term="cash budget">cash budget</button>;
- investment appraisal using <button data-ac-term="payback period">payback</button>, <button data-ac-term="accounting rate of return">ARR</button>, <button data-ac-term="net present value">NPV</button> and <button data-ac-term="internal rate of return">IRR</button>;
- working-capital decisions involving inventory, <button data-ac-term="economic order quantity">EOQ</button>, JIT and receivables;
- qualitative judgement alongside calculation.

## Interview carry-out

The professional value of this module is not “I once did accounting calculations.” It is the ability to explain how a business turns financial information into a decision.

A strong one-minute answer is:

> “Accounting for Financial Decision Making was mainly management accounting and finance. I learned to separate relevant from irrelevant costs, calculate contribution and break-even, deal with scarce resources, build cash budgets, appraise investments using payback, ARR, NPV and IRR, and manage working-capital decisions such as inventory and receivables. The main thing I take from it is that a calculation is only useful if you know which cash flows matter, what assumptions you are making, and what non-financial factors could change the decision.”

That answer can then be specialised for a corporate-services, finance, analytics or asset-management interview.
