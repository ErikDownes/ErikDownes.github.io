## Module overview

Complex Analysis extends calculus from real-valued functions to functions of a **complex variable**. The module focuses on the special structure of complex-differentiable functions and on integration in the complex plane.

## UL module overview

**Syllabus**

- Review of the basics
- Multivalued functions
- Analytic functions, the Cauchy–Riemann equations, Cauchy's theorem
- Integration on the complex plane

**Recommended textbooks**

- E. Kreyszig, *Advanced Engineering Mathematics*, 10th edition
- D. G. Zill and P. D. Shanahan, *A First Course in Complex Analysis*
- M. J. Ablowitz and A. S. Fokas, *Complex Variables: Introduction and Applications*, 2nd edition

- Multivalued functions

- Cauchy's theorem
- Integration in the complex plane

## Key definitions

- **Analytic function:** a function that is complex-differentiable throughout a neighbourhood.
- **Multivalued function:** a complex relation that can produce more than one value unless a branch is chosen.
- **Contour:** a path in the complex plane along which a complex integral is evaluated.

## Important methods / theorems

- Testing analyticity using the **Cauchy–Riemann equations**
- Working with branches of multivalued functions
- Evaluating contour integrals
- Applying **Cauchy's theorem**

## Explain it simply

Complex analysis is calculus carried out with complex numbers. The striking feature is that differentiability is much more restrictive than in ordinary real calculus, and that extra structure leads to powerful results for functions and integrals.

## Applications & connections

Complex analysis connects naturally with differential equations, fluid mechanics, waves, electrical engineering, signal methods and other areas of applied mathematics. For interview purposes, the useful point is that it develops rigorous mathematical reasoning and the ability to work with unfamiliar abstract structures.

## Interview questions

- What is an analytic function?
- What do the Cauchy–Riemann equations tell you?
- Why are complex numbers useful beyond pure mathematics?
- What is different about integration in the complex plane?
- How would you explain Cauchy's theorem at a high level?
