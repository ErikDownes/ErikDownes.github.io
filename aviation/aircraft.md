## Turboprops | Why do turboprops still matter in a jet age?



&nbsp;

Turboprops trade some speed for efficiency on shorter sectors. Rather than aviation simply progressing from propeller to jet, **the right propulsion system depends on the route and the job the aircraft has to do.**

## Regional Aviation | Why do regional routes favour turboprops?

Regional routes are often **shorter and lower-density**, where frequency and trip cost matter more than maximum speed.

Turboprops can offer:

- right-sized capacity;
- lower trip costs on suitable short sectors;
- access to smaller airports, including many with shorter runways;
- frequent services without requiring large passenger volumes.

**Remember:** the route drives the aircraft choice.

## ATR | What are the ATR 42 and ATR 72?

The **ATR 42** is the smaller aircraft for thinner regional routes; the **ATR 72** provides greater capacity where demand supports it.

Successive **-500 and -600 generations** modernised the families while retaining the same basic regional-aircraft proposition.

## Aircraft Choice | What determines which aircraft fits a route?

Aircraft choice combines:

- sector length;
- passenger demand and frequency;
- airport and runway constraints;
- fuel, maintenance and operating costs;
- acquisition or lease cost;
- reliability, support and residual value.

On short routes, extra jet speed may save relatively little total journey time. The commercial question is therefore:

**Which aircraft produces the best overall economics for this route?**
