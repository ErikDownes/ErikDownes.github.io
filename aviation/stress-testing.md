## What stress testing means

Stress testing asks what happens when important assumptions move against you. Instead of relying on one forecast, you deliberately test less favourable conditions and see whether the investment, lease or portfolio still works.

Sensitivity analysis usually changes one variable at a time. Scenario analysis changes several related assumptions together. A stress test goes further and asks what happens under a deliberately severe but plausible set of conditions.

## What could be stressed in an aircraft lease

For a simplified aircraft-leasing model, useful variables include lease rate, discount rate, residual value, aircraft downtime, maintenance cost, transition cost and the length of time needed to place the aircraft with another operator.

Credit conditions can also be stressed. An airline might pay late, require restructuring or default. The aircraft remains a physical asset, but repossession, transition, maintenance and remarketing can create cost and lost lease income.

## Base, downside and upside

A **base case** represents the central working assumptions. A **downside case** might combine weaker lease income, a higher discount rate, faster value decline and longer downtime. An **upside case** might assume stronger lease income, lower downtime and better residual value.

The purpose is not to claim that one scenario will happen. It is to understand the range of outcomes and identify which assumptions create the greatest exposure.

## NPV and stress testing

**NPV — Net Present Value — converts future cash flows into today's money using a discount rate.** In a lease model, future lease income and the aircraft's residual value can be discounted back to the present and compared with costs.

A higher discount rate reduces the present value of future cash flows. Lower lease income reduces cash coming in. A lower residual value reduces what the aircraft may be worth at the end of the model. Combining those changes gives a simple downside stress test.

## Why it matters in asset management

Aircraft are long-life assets, while market conditions, airline credit, interest rates, maintenance requirements and demand can change during a lease. Stress testing helps an asset manager understand where the risk sits before relying on a headline return.

It also encourages better questions: How much downtime can the case absorb? How dependent is the valuation on residual value? What happens if lease income falls? Which assumption changes the decision?
