# What do you know about Asset Management?

Asset management is compliment leasing.  Its about protecting the value and condition of your aircrafts and being clear on who pays for iit and keeps clear explicit records.  
  
It is  role made for me. 

The lessor owns a very valuable asset, but somebody else is operating it every day. So you need to know that the lessee is keeping their side of the agreement — maintaining the aircraft correctly, reporting properly, keeping the technical records complete, and returning the aircraft in the condition required by the lease.

I suppose there is a very simple analogy with a car. If I leased you a car, I wouldn't just care that you made the monthly payment. I'd want to know it was being serviced at the correct intervals and that there was a complete service history. With an aircraft, obviously, that becomes vastly more technical and financially significant.

I've been learning about **flight hours, cycles, engine shop visits, maintenance reserves and aircraft records**. With regional aircraft, cycles are particularly important because an ATR might take off and land six or eight times in a day.

One thing I found particularly interesting is the newer **PW127XT engine on the ATR -600s**. Pratt & Whitney quote about **40% more time on wing, taking the overhaul interval to 20,000 hours**, which means fewer engine events and lower maintenance cost.

And then you see why this matters commercially. Abelo has aircraft operating somewhere like the **Maldives**, where the ATR 42-600 is being used to connect an island network. Leasing allows an airline to access a modern aircraft without necessarily tying up the capital required to purchase it outright.

So I see asset management as this constant interaction between **operations, maintenance, contractual compliance and valuation**.

One thing I'd actually be interested to ask you is: **when an engine has to come off for a shop visit, how do you manage that in practice? Would the airline normally have access to a spare or pooled engine so that the airframe can remain in service, rather than having the whole aircraft grounded?**
