## Why Aviation?

I’ve always had a fascination with flight. Every time I fly, I still find it remarkable that **engineering, mathematics, science and technology all come together to put an aircraft in the air safely and reliably**.

But preparing for this role over the last week or so has given me a completely different perspective on aviation. I’ve started to understand the **aircraft as both an engineering asset and a financial asset**, and that really connects with my background in Financial Mathematics and Data Analysis.

What particularly interests me about asset management is how **multivariable** it is. There are obvious quantitative factors — lease rates, maintenance costs, utilisation, residual values and cash flows — but the categorical information can be just as important: aircraft type, engine configuration, maintenance status, operator, geography and contractual conditions.

And then there is the human side. Aircraft leasing depends on **long-term relationships and trust between lessor and lessee**. Abelo talks about being an *active and engaged partner* and developing tailor-made solutions for airlines. That combination of **data, finance, aircraft and relationships** is what has made me genuinely excited about the role.

The more I’ve learned about regional aviation and turboprops in particular, **the more interested I’ve become**. I didn't just want to arrive at the interview saying I was interested in aviation — I wanted to do the work and understand the business.
