## Why Aviation?

I’ve always had a fascination with flight. Every time I fly, I still find it incredible that engineering, mathematics, science and technology all come together to put an aircraft in the air safely and reliably.

But preparing for this role over the last week or so has given me a completely different perspective on aviation. I’ve started to see the aircraft as both an engineering asset and a financial asset, and that really connects with my background in Financial Mathematics and Data Analysis.

What really interests me about asset management is how many things come together. You have the numbers — lease rates, maintenance costs, utilisation, residual values and cash flows — but you also have the aircraft type, its condition, the operator, where it is flying and the lease agreement itself.

Then there’s the human side. Aircraft leasing depends on long-term relationships and trust between the lessor and the airline. I noticed that Abelo talks about being an active and engaged partner and finding solutions for its customers.

That combination of aviation, data, finance and relationships is what has really attracted me to the role. The more I’ve learned about regional aviation and turboprops, the more interested I’ve become.

I didn’t want to arrive at the interview just saying I was interested in aviation. I wanted to actually do the work and start understanding the business.
