## Preparation | Understand the task before starting

I want to know what decision the work supports, what information is available, who will use the result and when it is needed. I break the task into manageable steps and check the source before relying on a figure.

## Clarity | Communicate for the audience

A useful explanation starts with the point the listener needs. I aim to use plain language, show the evidence behind a conclusion and give more technical detail when it helps. In writing, I use tools, including AI, to improve a draft while taking responsibility for the final meaning.

## Expectations | Agree what good work looks like

I would clarify the deadline, format and level of detail early. If a dependency or obstacle threatens the deadline, I would raise it while there is still time to adjust the plan. Clear expectations make teamwork and independent work easier.

## Work Ethic | Finish and check the work

A calculation, report or dashboard is useful only if someone can rely on it. I want to check inputs, test whether the result makes sense, document important assumptions and make the next step clear.

## Integrity | Say what is known and what is uncertain

I would distinguish a verified fact from an estimate or an assumption. If I found an error, I would correct it and tell the people affected. Trust depends on making limitations visible, especially when the work may inform a decision.
